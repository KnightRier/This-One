Dreo Just Recalled 375,000+ Space Heaters for Fire Risk — A Real Problem If You're Using One Near a Grow Tent or Balcony Setup This Winter

2026-11-02

Disclosure: this page contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, Small Space Grow may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. We only recommend products based on our own research.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission announced a recall of Dreo's DR-HSH015 "Whole Room Heater 715" on October 29, 2026, covering roughly 338,800 units sold in the U.S. and another 36,300 in Canada — units made between April 2025 and January 2026 can overheat and ignite. Dreo has logged 17 reports of units catching fire, three with property damage and one with a burn injury. For most apartments this is a straightforward recall story, but it lands at exactly the moment a lot of apartment and balcony growers are reaching for a small space heater to keep a grow tent, propagation shelf, or marginal balcony setup above freezing — and a space heater tucked next to a plastic grow tent, paper pots, or a stack of seed-starting mix is a materially worse place for a fire-risk unit to be than a living room floor.

Check your heater against the actual recall details before assuming it's fine

The recall is narrow and specific, not a blanket ban on Dreo heaters: it's only the DR-HSH015 model, and only units with a date code (printed on a label on the underside, in YYYY/MM format) between 2025/04 and 2026/01. These were sold roughly September 2025 through October 2026 for $70-$110, at Costco stores and Costco.com, plus BestBuy.com, Kohls.com, Lowes.com, QVC.com, HomeDepot.com, and B2BRenew.com — so if you bought a small Dreo heater online in the last year to warm a growing area, this is worth five minutes to check rather than assume it doesn't apply to you. If your unit matches, the remedy is not a simple firmware fix: Dreo is telling owners to stop using it immediately, unplug it, cut the power cord, and send the company a photo of the cut cord to get a refund. Their recall contact line is 833-820-0887. Don't keep it running "until the replacement arrives" — that's exactly the usage pattern behind the incidents already reported.

Why this matters more in a grow tent than in a living room

A fire-risk space heater is dangerous anywhere, but the way apartment growers actually use these things raises the stakes: a heater run inside or right next to an enclosed grow tent sits close to reflective mylar, plastic zippers, and often an extension cord or power strip already loaded down with a light timer and a humidifier. A living-room heater that overheats has open air around it and a smoke detector nearby; a heater wedged behind a tent flap to keep a propagation setup above 60°F overnight does not, and it's rarely something you're watching when it happens. If you're using any space heater — recalled or not — to protect plants in a tent, closet, or sealed-off balcony corner this winter, this is a good prompt to actually check that it has tip-over shutoff and overheat protection (most legitimate heaters list this explicitly in the spec sheet), and to never run one on a daisy-chained power strip that's also carrying grow lights.
