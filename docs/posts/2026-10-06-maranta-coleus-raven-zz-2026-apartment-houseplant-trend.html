Weather.com's plant desk just published its pick for "the 3 plants set to be the biggest gardening trend of 2026," and the list is a pointed contrast to the rare-collectible, humidity-cabinet plants this site covered earlier this month. The three named are maranta (the prayer plant), coleus, and the raven ZZ plant — and what they have in common, according to the report, isn't rarity or difficulty, it's the opposite: fast growth, low maintenance, and real visual payoff without specialized equipment. That matters for an apartment specifically because the other 2026 trend — ultra-rare variegated aroids that need 70% humidity cabinets — is genuinely hard to pull off in a dry, heated apartment in October. These three aren't. Each one does have a real tradeoff worth knowing before you buy, though, and none of them is "none."

The raven ZZ plant: almost impossible to kill, but only in low light — not neglect of everything

The 'Raven' cultivar of Zamioculcas zamiifolia starts out lime green and matures into glossy, near-black foliage, which is the whole reason it's showing up in garden centers and interior design features right now instead of the plain green ZZ that's been a houseplant-care cliché for a decade. The actual care profile is identical to a regular ZZ plant: it tolerates low light, needs infrequent watering, and stores water in its rhizomes, so missing a watering is far less dangerous than overwatering one. The cultivar tops out small — around 3 feet tall and wide — which keeps it realistic for a tabletop, nightstand, or a windowless bathroom corner, exactly the spots an apartment has in surplus and most trendy plants can't handle. The one real failure mode is root rot from a pot with no drainage combined with a well-meaning owner who waters on a fixed schedule instead of checking the soil first.

Maranta: the one of the three that actually fights your apartment's heating season

Maranta leuconeura, the prayer plant, earns its name because its leaves fold upward at night and flatten again in daylight — a visible, daily movement that's a genuine selling point, not marketing copy. The catch, and it's a real one for an apartment in October: maranta wants meaningfully higher humidity than the 20-30% a heated apartment typically runs once the radiators or forced-air heat kick on for the season, which this site already covered as the actual cause of most fall houseplant stress. Skip the humidity and you get crisping leaf edges, not a dead plant — it's a cosmetic problem, not a fatal one, but it defeats the point of buying a plant specifically for its leaf display. A cheap hygrometer removes the guesswork about whether a given spot in the apartment is actually dry enough to matter.

Coleus indoors is the surprise of the three — it wants what an apartment window already has

Coleus has spent decades as an outdoor bedding plant, which makes its appearance on a 2026 houseplant trend list the most unexpected of the three. Grown indoors, its actual requirement is bright, indirect light — an east- or north-facing window works, with the common failure being too little light rather than too much, which dulls the leaf color that's the entire reason to grow it. It wants a consistent temperature range of roughly 60-75°F and soil kept lightly moist, never bone-dry and never soggy — both of which describe a typical heated apartment interior almost exactly, which is the actual reason it translates indoors better than its outdoor reputation suggests. The visual payoff is real: coleus cultivars come in leaf patterns and colors (reds, purples, chartreuse) that most low-light apartment houseplants simply can't match.
