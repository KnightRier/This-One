The "All-In-One" Countertop Hydroponic Tower Isn't One Price — What Gardyn and Tower Garden Actually Cost After You Buy

2026-10-08

Disclosure: this page contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, Small Space Grow may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. We only recommend products based on our own research.

Indoor hydroponic towers are being pitched hard right now as the fix for apartment growing: no soil, no yard, no window required, just a countertop column that waters and feeds itself. What the marketing pages don't put up front is that the sticker price on two of the best-known systems, Gardyn and Tower Garden, isn't close to what you'll actually spend to grow anything in them. One leans on a recurring membership that keeps nudging you back in after you cancel; the other sells you a tower that can't grow indoors until you pay several hundred dollars more for the light kit it needs. Neither is a scam, exactly, but both are a worse deal than they look at first click — and for most apartment herb growers, a much cheaper combination covers the same need.

Gardyn's real cost is the membership, not the $899 unit

Gardyn's current Home unit is $899 up front (or financed at roughly $38/month), and the company is explicit that membership is technically optional. In practice the pricing is built to push you into it anyway: with an active membership, replacement plant pods ("yCubes") run $1.99 each; without one, the same pod jumps to $4.99 plus a flat $9.99 shipping charge per order, which turns a handful of replacement herbs into a $30+ purchase. The membership itself is $39/month, or $408 for a one-year term, or $686 for two years — money that buys plant credits, the AI-driven "Kelby" grow assistant, and premium app features, but keeps running whether or not you're actively using the tower that month. Do the math before buying: a two-year membership alone is more than three-quarters of the unit's purchase price.

Tower Garden's base price doesn't include the part that makes it work indoors

Tower Garden HOME lists at $725 on the manufacturer's own site — but that price is for the "no lights" version, built for a spot with strong direct sun like a sunroom or south-facing balcony. For an actual apartment interior, you need the "with Lights" bundle, which runs $1,020 — about $295 more just to add the LED fixture that makes the tower usable away from a window. That's a markup worth checking against buying a standalone grow light separately, especially since the tower's own frame can usually accommodate a clamp-on or add-on panel instead of the bundled one.

For most apartment herb growers, skip the tower entirely

Both systems are solving a real problem — consistent watering and light without a yard — but a hydroponic tower's main advantage is vertical growing capacity for a dozen-plus plants at once, which is more than most apartment kitchens need for a rotation of basil, mint, and a couple of lettuces. A basic self-watering planter under a clamp grow light gets you the same consistent-moisture, consistent-light result for a fraction of either system's total cost, with no proprietary pods and no membership to remember to cancel. The tower setups earn their price for someone who wants a genuinely large indoor harvest and doesn't want to manage multiple separate pots; for a windowsill or counter rotation of herbs, the stripped-down version does the same job.

Neither Gardyn nor Tower Garden is misrepresenting what their hardware does — the complaint is what the advertised price leaves out. Gardyn's $899 sticker doesn't mention that skipping the $39/month membership makes every replacement pod cost roughly 2.5x more, and Tower Garden's $725 HOME price is for a unit that can't grow anything indoors until you add the $295 light kit. Both are legitimate choices for someone who wants a large, low-effort indoor harvest and is willing to pay for the convenience. But if what you actually need is a few pots of herbs on a counter, a cheap self-watering planter and a clamp-on grow light bulb deliver the same consistent water-and-light result for well under $100 total, with nothing recurring to cancel later.
