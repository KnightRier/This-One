Most of the fall houseplant advice going around right now is about protecting plants from cold and low light — bringing things in before frost, adding a grow light after the equinox. Holiday cacti (Schlumbergera, sold as Christmas or Thanksgiving cactus) work the opposite way: they need a deliberate stretch of cool nights or extended darkness in fall to set flower buds at all, and extension guidance from Clemson's Home & Garden Information Center and NC State Cooperative Extension both point to roughly six to eight weeks of that treatment before bloom. Counting back from Thanksgiving (November 26) or Christmas puts the start of that window right now, in late September — not a vague 'do this sometime this fall' instruction. An apartment with a consistently cool windowsill or porch may already be doing this by accident; one that isn't needs to start the dark-period routine this week to have any real shot at holiday blooms rather than a plant that just sits green.

Bud set needs cool nights OR a strict dark period — figure out which one your space can actually do

Per Clemson HGIC, Christmas and Thanksgiving cacti will set flower buds under either of two conditions: cool night temperatures of roughly 50-55°F sustained over several weeks, or, if the plant is staying warmer than that, 13 hours of fully uninterrupted darkness nightly (at 55-70°F) or 15 hours of darkness (above 70°F) — with both approaches needing to run for about six to eight weeks before bloom. A drafty apartment windowsill or an enclosed, unheated porch or balcony often already sits in that 50-55°F night range in late September and does the job with zero extra effort, so the first move is actually checking your specific spot's overnight low rather than assuming you need a routine at all. A cheap digital min/max thermometer left in the exact spot for a few nights gives you that real number instead of a guess, and tells you whether you can skip the dark-period routine entirely or need to commit to it.

No cool spot? The dark-period method works, but the failure mode is a hallway light, not the technique

If your apartment stays warm all night, the alternative is enforcing 13-16 hours of genuinely uninterrupted darkness every night for six to eight weeks — covering the plant with an opaque cloth or moving it into a closet each evening and back out each morning, per Clemson HGIC and NC State Extension guidance. The most common way this fails isn't the concept, it's a hallway light, a TV, or a streetlight through a gap in the cover breaking the dark cycle on some nights but not others, which resets progress without you noticing since the plant won't show any sign of it for weeks. A dedicated opaque blackout cloth that fully seals around the pot is more reliable for this than a cardboard box or a blanket that shifts overnight, and it's reusable every fall once you own one.

Once buds actually appear, the plant needs stability, not more attention

Starting late is also fixable: if you don't already have a holiday cactus, buying one now that's still a few weeks from bud set lets you begin the same routine immediately rather than waiting another year. Once buds do show up — from either method — the guidance flips to protecting them: keep the plant in the same spot at a steady 60-70°F, since Clemson HGIC and multiple extension sources note that moving the pot, cold drafts from a window or heating-vent blasts, and sudden watering swings are the leading causes of bud drop after the hard part is already done. Water more frequently once buds set, but not with more volume than usual, and stop fertilizing until after it finishes blooming.
