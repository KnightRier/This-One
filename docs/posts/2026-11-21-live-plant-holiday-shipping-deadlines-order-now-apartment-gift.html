Why plant sellers cut themselves off weeks before FedEx or UPS do

A standard package sitting in a sorting facility for an extra day doesn't change state; a live plant or bulb does. Growers build in a buffer for slower holiday transit times, extra handling at distribution hubs, and the chance that a package sits on a cold porch for a day before anyone brings it in — all things a sweater shipment shrugs off and a rooted plant doesn't. That's why a retailer like Holland Bulb Farms sets its amaryllis cutoff at December 5 even though FedEx and UPS are still accepting packages for Christmas arrival through December 24 — the carrier's delivery promise and the plant's chance of surviving the trip in good shape are two different numbers, and the plant seller is the one who knows the difference.

If you're ordering a gift plant, build your own calendar backward from December 5
