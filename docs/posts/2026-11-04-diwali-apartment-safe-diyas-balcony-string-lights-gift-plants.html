Diwali runs Dhanteras on November 6 through the main Lakshmi Puja on the evening of Sunday, November 8, with Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj following November 9-10 — so if you're decorating a rented apartment this year, the clock is already down to days, not weeks. The gap between a house with a driveway and a unit with a lease is bigger than most Diwali guides admit: most US apartment leases and fire codes prohibit open flame indoors and on balconies outright, and balconies usually have no exterior outlet for the string lights everyone's porch seems to have. None of that means skipping the holiday — it means swapping two specific things before Sunday, and picking a gift plant that'll actually survive the apartment it's going into.

Real diyas and a rental lease don't mix — and fire departments have been saying so for years

Oil diyas and tea-light candles are an unattended open flame sitting on a shrine or windowsill for hours, which is exactly the setup fire brigades call out every Diwali season: UK fire services including the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea and Derbyshire Fire & Rescue specifically urge swapping real candles for flameless LED versions, after incidents traced to a single tea light left burning near a shrine. NFPA data on US home fires puts candles at roughly 2-3% of all reported home fires and a similar share of fire deaths — a small overall slice, but nearly all of it comes from candles left burning unattended in exactly the way a diya display sits for an evening. If your lease has a no-open-flame clause (most do), a battery-powered LED diya set gets you the look for the actual Lakshmi Puja evening without the clause violation or the risk.

Flameless LED tea-light diyas, battery-operated, set of 12+

The specific swap fire-safety guidance points to: same warm flicker and shape as an oil diya, no flame, no smoke, and nothing to blow out or knock over near curtains or a balcony railing. Buy a set rather than singles — Diwali displays use far more than you'd think once you're lining a windowsill or doorway.

Battery tea-light sets are one of the cheaper decor categories in this niche, usually sold in bulk packs.

The balcony string-light problem nobody plans for until the day of

Outdoor string lights are the other Diwali staple, and the other thing that quietly fails in an apartment: most balconies have no GFCI exterior outlet, and running an extension cord through a sliding door is both a tripping hazard and against most lease fire-safety terms. The fix fire-safety guidance for apartment dwellers converges on is the same one covered on patios with no outlet access generally — battery-powered or USB-rechargeable LED string lights with a built-in timer, which clip to a railing with zip ties or clips instead of nails, and don't need a run to an indoor outlet at all.

Battery-powered LED string lights with timer, warm white

Runs 6-8 hours a night on batteries or a USB power bank instead of a cord to an indoor outlet — the practical fix for a balcony railing with no exterior outlet, which describes most rental balconies. The built-in timer means you're not the one remembering to turn it off at 1am.

If you're gifting a plant instead of sweets this year, pick one that survives the apartment it's going to

Money plant (Epipremnum aureum, sold in the US simply as pothos) and peace lily are the two houseplants that actually show up as Diwali gifts for a reason beyond symbolism: pothos tolerates the low, indirect light of a typical apartment living room and forgives a missed watering, while peace lily is one of the few low-light plants that will still flower indoors — worth it for a gift that's meant to look festive, not just green. The mismatch that kills most gifted plants fast is the one nobody warns the recipient about: a plant that arrives in a nursery pot with no drainage reservoir gets overwatered by someone who doesn't know its schedule yet. A self-watering pot removes that specific failure point for the first few months, which is exactly when a gifted plant is most likely to get killed by good intentions.

Self-watering planter pot, 6-8" (for pothos or peace lily)

Built-in reservoir and water-level indicator so a recipient who's never owned the plant before can't easily overwater it in the first few weeks — the main way a gifted low-light houseplant actually dies.

Inexpensive relative to the plant itself; sized for a standard 6" nursery pot of pothos or a young peace lily.

The through-line for an apartment Diwali is the same constraint three times over: no open flame, no outdoor outlet, no guesswork for whoever's on the receiving end of a gift. Flameless LED diyas and battery string lights solve the first two before Sunday evening without touching your lease terms, and a self-watering pot under a pothos or peace lily solves the third for whoever you're gifting it to. None of it requires a house or a yard — it requires having the flameless versions in hand by Saturday, not Sunday afternoon.
