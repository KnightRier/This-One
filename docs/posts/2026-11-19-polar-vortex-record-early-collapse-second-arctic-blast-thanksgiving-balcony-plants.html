The eastern US already felt a preview of the cold that broke loose from it. Now forecasters are watching for a second wave that could arrive around Thanksgiving weekend (November 26-29) — but how strong it gets is genuinely unsettled. MIT's Judah Cohen told Gizmodo a return to wintry conditions is likely but it's "TBD" how severe, and other meteorologists have said their models are specifically struggling with the Thanksgiving window. That's a different situation from the first-freeze records and clear-cut cold snaps earlier this month: this is a probable but unconfirmed cold event landing on exactly the weekend apartment and condo dwellers are most likely to be traveling and unable to react in real time.

If you're traveling for Thanksgiving, cover what stays outside before you leave — not after

Potted plants that have been riding out the mild stretches on a balcony or patio are the ones at risk here, and the problem with a probable-but-uncertain forecast is that the window to act is before you leave for the holiday, not after you're already gone and checking the weather from someone else's living room. A floating row cover or frost blanket thrown loosely over a pot (not cinched tight against the stem) traps a few degrees of radiant heat overnight without needing power or water, which matters if the second wave does show up while nobody's home to bring anything inside. It's also cheap and reusable enough to just leave on for the whole holiday stretch rather than gambling on timing it precisely.

The models disagree with each other — so don't pre-decide, give yourself a remote switch instead

Because the forecasters themselves are flagging low confidence on severity and timing, the more useful prep isn't guessing right, it's not needing to guess at all. Running a heat cable, a small plant-safe heat mat, or even an outdoor string of incandescent lights under the frost cover, plugged into a WiFi smart plug, means you can turn supplemental warmth on from your phone if the cold actually arrives on schedule — and leave it off, saving power, if the models were wrong and it doesn't. That turns an uncertain multi-day forecast into something you can react to in real time instead of committing to before you leave town.
